Chicago Crime Arrests by District

The graph below shows the number of arrests reported in each police district from 2001-present. Click on any district for a more detailed analysis of the number of arrests made each year.

Selected Year Range: All years

Note that, for the purposes of rendering the map in a reasonable amount of time and having responsive interaction, the map only displays a small subset of the original data. Some districts (such as 31 and 61) do not have any observations in this subset.

WRITE UP:

Introduction:

The dataset used is the Chicago Crimes dataset. Each item in the data is either a reported incident or a record for each victim of a murder. Crime Type, Arrest Status, and Year were selected to provide more detail about each crime. Each of these variables can be filtered on in the visualizations above. Latitude, Longitude, Community Area, and Police District were used as geographical variables. Latitude and Longitude are used to plot observations on the map, and the Community Area is listed in tooltips as the GeoJSON used breaks Chicago down by community areas. In the bar chart, arrests are broken down by Police District in order to investigate differing arrest trends and behaviors across districts.

Rationale:

Dynamic filters and queries were implemented with dropdowns and sliders for the variables listed above.
Panning and zooming was implemented on the map, and tooltips were implemented for each mark on the map.

Panning and zooming were obvious choices for an interaction with the map.
Due to there being too many different values for district, community area, and crime type, tooltips were chosen to communicate these values rather than trying to find different colors or shapes for each unique value of these attributes.
A map was chosen in the first place as it is one of the quickest and most effective ways to illustrate the geographical distribution of the data. From there, the above interactions emerged naturally.
Fill was used to encode arrest status on the map, as it seemed more effective than shape. Shape may have required more focus to discern and overlapping points may have interfered with identifying the shape of a given mark. Since arrest is a boolean value, fill works well for this attribute.
Color was used to denote points selected by brushing. Since the point of brushing was to link to data on the other visualization that only encoded arrests, only points that have true as an arrest value are highlighted.
